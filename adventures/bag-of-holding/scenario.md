## Introduction

You wake up. The last thing you remember is a night out on the
town. Now you seem to be in a giant cave made of... canvas?

## RPG Mechanics

The 5e system is used for stat checks and combat.  Skill checks are
supported (they are stat checks naming a skill); see the default
character below for the Rogue's proficiencies.

The player's inventory is initially empty (if an initial character
sheet was supplied, the inventory is cleared out).

If no player stats are supplied, use the following defaults:

Class: Rogue
Race: Human
Level: 4
Attributes: STR 10, DEX 13, CON 12, INT 11, WIS 10, CHA 10
Proficiency Bonus: +2
HP: 27
AC: 11 (unarmored + Dex bonus)
Saving Throws: DEX, INT
Skill Proficiencies: Acrobatics, Arcana, Athletics, Deception,
Insight, Investigation, Perception, Persuasion, Sleight of Hand,
Stealth

The adventure is written for a Rogue and rewards the Rogue's bag of
tricks: Stealth, Sleight of Hand, Investigation, and a silver tongue.
A player with a different class simply lacks the +2 proficiency on
those checks; the DCs stay the same.

## Rooms

### Axe Head (START)

The player starts out on what turns out to be the head of a giant
battleaxe.  The axe head leans at an angle against the wall of the
still-larger cave (this will turn out to be the inside of a Bag of
Holding).  The handle is about the size of a narrow sidewalk, sloping
downward into the murk.

There's a very faint luminescence coming from the walls, which seems
to be the main thing preventing this area from being pitch black.
Here at the top of the bag the glow is at its faintest, a sourceless
cold blue that does nothing to warm the air.  When examined, an INT
check (DC 12, non-repeatable) lets the player deduce that this glow is
magical in nature (it's a side effect of the Bag's magic).

Above, just above head height, the walls converge into a low dome,
scrunching shut in the center.

The top of the blade has made a small rip in the fabric.  The player
can poke their head out or even try to squeeze through (see The Rip,
Mechanics section).

The player can drop down into the darkness below (see Dropping
mechanic below), or clamber carefully down the axe handle.

### Axe Handle (Upper)

Here, a mass of sticky webs is attached to the giant axe handle,
greatly diminishing the glow from the canvas walls.  The player can
barely make out their way, balancing carefully on the handle.  The
glow here is dimmer and sicklier, a gray-green filtered through the
web.

The sloping handle continues upward and downward.  The webs seem to be
denser on the lower side.  It's also possible to drop down into the
darkness below, but it's too dark to see where one would land (see
Dropping, in the Mechanics section).

There are various strange masses, of different sizes, stuck in the
webs.  If the player examines these masses, or the web itself, they
turn up a stuck FLY, which can talk and is about the size of a dog
relative to the player.  All the other stuck objects are too tightly
wrapped in webbing to identify or extricate at a glance -- but one of
the larger masses, on *rigorous* examination, turns out to be a
desiccated shrunken person: a previous prisoner (see The Prisoner's
Husk, Mechanics section).

If the player has not noticed the FLY yet, the groaning sounds and/or
warning messages are narrated as coming from somewhere in the web; the
narrator should not reveal the FLY until the player does an
examination.

### Axe Handle (Lower)

Here, the webs surrounding the giant axe handle are very dense.  If
the player wants to continue upward or downward along the path, they
have to get through them first (see Web in the Mechanics section).
The glow is nearly swallowed by the web here; the air smells of old
dust and something faintly sweet.

The SPIDER is concealed in the shadows above.  If the spider has not
been revealed, a successful WIS (Perception) check (DC 13, repeatable)
each time the player enters the room or examines the web, lets the
player notice its presence.  For details, see SPIDER (NPCs section).

Peering over the side of the axe handle, the player can make out
numerous irregularly-shaped objects below.  It seems possible to drop
down safely from here.

The player can also look down at what lies beneath.  If the player has
not yet visited the bag floor, passing a WIS check (DC 11, repeatable)
lets them discern giant versions of empty potion bottles, copper
pieces, corks, moldy sandwiches, piled on each other.  If KORBAR (see
NPCs) is alive, the player can also hear a faint clanking and
muttering from below.  If the player has already visited the bag
floor, the WIS check is not necessary.

### Bag Floor

The floor of the cave is covered with a loose pile of giant-sized
rubbish.  From the glow of the canvas walls, the player can make out
copper pieces, empty potion bottles, used corks, lint, moldy
sandwiches, and other more disgusting items.  The glow is warmest
here, but the smell is worst: rot, dust, and stale wine all at once.
For details about the rubbish, which conceals some important items,
see the Mechanics section below.

The base of the giant axe rests in the center of the pile.  It's
possible to clamber back up the handle from here.

Here is KORBAR, a female Dwarf, drunk and miserable, wearing
ridiculously noisy platemail (see NPCs section).  She sits beneath a
makeshift shelter made from a giant overturned soup ladle (see
Korbar's Shelter, Mechanics section).

### Secret Pocket

This room, about the size of a small closet, is accessed by squeezing
through a concealed flap in the floor of the Bag of Holding.  Its
walls are made of the same faintly-glowing canvas as the rest of the
cave, but even stickier and smellier.  The smell here is the worst in
the whole bag -- the pocket is the oldest part of it.

The room holds a large iron KEY (see Win Condition), a sewn-in
instruction label (see The Bag's Instruction Label), a sending stone
(see The Sending Stone), and a hip flask of emergency gin (see
Emergency Gin).

## Mechanics

### The Rip

There is a small rip in the canvas wall of the Bag of Holding,
accessible from the Axe Head (starting room).

While in this room, the player can walk carefully to the tip of the
blade and reach the rip.  Upon sticking their head through it
(equivalent to an Examine action), they see the following:

- The area outside is a gray nothing.  Upon seeing this, a successful
  INT check (DC 12, non-repeatable) lets the player recognize this
  nothingness as the Astral Plane.

- Looking around, they see that the rip is actually a hole in the wall
  of what looks like a giant sack, or bag.  Above, the neck of the Bag
  is firmly shut by a giant padlock, which dangles down to their
  level.  The keyhole is barely within reach.

- If the previous INT check succeeded, passing another INT check (DC
  17, non-repeatable) lets the player realize what the bag is.  It's a
  Bag of Holding -- a legendary magical storage item that works by
  existing in the prime and astral planes simultaneously.

- Once the player knows they are looking at the Astral Plane, a later
  examination can catch a glimpse of something vast drifting past in
  the gray: a shape like a mountain made of silence, or a whale made
  of stars.  It does not notice the player.  The player is glad.  (A
  wonder-and-menace beat; no check, no mechanical effect.)

The above features (the gray void, the bag exterior, the padlock, the
astral drifter) should not be narrated until the player has looked out
the rip via an explicit examination action.  From the main part of the
room, they are not visible, though the narrator can note the dull gray
light winking through the rip.

Any item dropped through the rip disappears into the gray void.  If
the player drops the giant key (see below) through it, they are
trapped forever (game over).

The player can try to squeeze their whole body through the rip.  This
requires a DEX (Acrobatics) check (DC 12, non-repeatable).  If the
check succeeds, the GM should cancel the action and ask the player if
they're sure.  If they repeat the action, then they proceed to squeeze
through the rip (no additional check is needed), and come to a grisly
end floating eternally in the Astral Plane (game over).

### Dropping from the axe handle

If the player jumps down from the Axe Head, they reach the Bag Floor
(to the astonishment of KORBAR if she's present), but sprain an ankle
badly: lose 1d4 DEX, 1d4 CON, and 3d6 HP.  (The stat losses are
permanent.)

If dropping from the Axe Handle (Upper), they reach the Bag Floor but
take 2d6 damage (no stat loss).

If dropping from the Axe Handle (Lower), they're momentarily winded
but otherwise uninjured.

### Web

On the Axe Handle (Lower), any attempt by the player to proceed (i.e.,
going down if coming from above, going up if coming from below)
requires them to first get through the web.  For returning in the
direction from which they came, there is no impedance.

There are three ways through the web, and they differ in noise:

1. **Force through (loud).**  A successful STR (Athletics) check (DC
   14 without a weapon, DC 10 if armed with a blade-like weapon --
   the toenail sword, a dagger, the giant lockpick, or anything else
   the GM deems usable -- both repeatable).  Once the web is cleared,
   no check is needed for subsequent passage.  However, any attempt to
   force through the web while the SPIDER is present, whether
   successful or unsuccessful, triggers the SPIDER attack encounter
   and cancels traversal (see NPCs).  The web transmits vibrations:
   this is *why* the spider always detects forcing.  The GM should
   narrate the web shivering with the strain.

2. **Cut quietly (slow, careful).**  The player can instead work at
   the web with blade and patience: a DEX (Stealth) check (DC 13, or
   DC 10 with a blade; repeatable).  On success, the player parts the
   web strand by strand and opens the passage without the spider
   noticing -- the web is cleared, and the spider is not provoked.
   On failure, the player's careful work disturbs the web: the spider
   senses it and attacks (the encounter proceeds as in (1)).  While
   KORBAR is following the player in her plate armor, she imposes
   disadvantage on this check -- the clanking gives them away.  She
   will agree to doff the armor (see Korbar, NPCs) once she believes
   the spider is dead, or once she has magical protection.

3. **Walk through invisible.**  If the player is under the effect of
   the invisibility potion (see Potions), they can simply move through
   the web slowly and carefully: the passage opens (the web is
   cleared) and the spider senses nothing.  This auto-succeeds while
   invisible -- invisibility plus care is the surest possible path
   past the spider.  (Invisibility alone, with a careless approach,
   does not guarantee silence: the web still transmits vibrations, and
   a fumbled attempt still provokes the spider.)

The player may try to search the web.  On the Axe Handle (Upper), the
first examine action turns up the FLY (see NPCs).  With a rigorous
examination, one larger mass turns out to be a previous prisoner (see
The Prisoner's Husk).  On the Axe Handle (Lower), searching the web
once the SPIDER is dead, departed, or asleep turns up the spider's
hoard (see The Spider's Hoard).  The web itself is too sticky to do
anything useful with.

### Rubbish

The bottom of the bag of holding is covered with a random collection
of rubbish: copper pieces, empty potion bottles, used corks, lint,
moldy sandwiches, etc., all giant-sized.  [Implementation note: the
scenario generator should pre-generate a collection of plausible
items, possibly including humorous ones, and be prepared to accept
them into the narrative.]

Examining the rubbish or the room or any item within the rubbish (this
must be an explicit action, not just the room description produced
upon room entry) has the following effects:

- The player notices a giant toenail clipping within the pile, which
  can be wielded as a sword.  Removing this from the loose pile
  requires a successful DEX check (DC 8); thereafter, the toenail
  functions as a shortsword (1d6 piercing, finesse, light).

- If the player passes a successful INT (Investigation) check (DC 8,
  non-repeatable), they realize this is not a random pile of objects:
  it's some adventurer's supplies.  After all, their own (now missing)
  pack contained much the same assortment of items.

- If the player has made the previous observation, *and* makes another
  explicit examination action, *and* does not yet know that they are
  in a Bag of Holding, *and* passes another INT check (DC 14,
  non-repeatable), they realize they are inside a magical Bag of
  Holding.  The player also notices that the proportions of the
  rubbish aren't all equal; it seems the Bag's magic shrinks items by
  different amounts to get them to fit.

- If the player searches *thoroughly*, and this is not the first
  examination (i.e., the toenail sword has already been noticed), and
  the giant handkerchief has not yet been revealed (see next section),
  then upon a successful WIS (Perception) check (DC 15,
  non-repeatable), they notice the giant handkerchief and suddenly get
  the feeling that it's somehow important.  (The faerie-fire scroll can
  reveal the handkerchief instead; see Scrolls.)

- **Deep rubbish -- stage one.**  On a rigorous examination with a
  successful INT (Investigation) check (DC 12, repeatable), the
  player digs deeper and turns up the *unbroken* finds: four giant
  potion bottles, each the size of a vat, their corks still in place,
  disambiguated by color (amber, emerald, crimson, ink-black); a wad
  of edible-if-revolting rations; and a giant lockpick, the size of a
  two-handed staff.  See Potions, Rations, and the Giant Lockpick.

- **Deep rubbish -- stage two.**  On a further rigorous examination
  with a successful INT (Investigation) check (DC 15, repeatable),
  the player uncovers a giant scroll, twice the player's height,
  rolled tight: a Scroll of Faerie Fire (see Scrolls).

- **Deep rubbish -- stage three.**  On the deepest rigorous
  examination, with a successful INT (Investigation) check (DC 17,
  repeatable), the player finds a tiny leather pouch, far too
  small to be one of the giant's belongings -- at the player's scale,
  it is exactly the size of a normal adventurer's Bag of Holding (see
  A Bag of Holding Within).

### Giant Handkerchief

This giant handkerchief is draped over a corner of the rubbish pile in
the bag floor.  It is filthy and disgustingly damp.

The handkerchief is not initially noticed by the player.  It is
revealed if (i) the player searches the rubbish and passes a WIS
(Perception) check (see Rubbish, above), (ii) KORBAR points it out
(see NPCs, below), or (iii) the faerie-fire scroll's light sweeps the
rubbish pile (see Scrolls).

Once the handkerchief is revealed, the player can lift it or move it
aside.  This reveals a small flap in the canvas floor underneath,
leading down into darkness (the Secret Pocket).

### Potions

The four giant potion bottles in the deep rubbish are each the size of
a vat relative to the player.  They cannot be carried; they must be
drunk from directly, leaning over the rim.  Their types are initially
unknown, and the bottle labels are worn and tiny.  The potions can be
identified three ways:

- **Read the fine print.**  An INT (Arcana) check (DC 12,
  non-repeatable) on a bottle deciphers its label.  (Arcane potions
  come with warnings; see below.)
- **Sip to identify.**  The classic ritual.  Uncorking a bottle
  requires a STR check (DC 10, repeatable).  A sip is a taste test:
  it identifies the potion, and costs nothing -- except on the
  ink-black bottle, whose single sip burns like swallowed lightning: a
  CON save (DC 11) or 1d8 poison damage, half on a success.
- **Drink blind.**  Dumb, but free.  Drinking a bottle fully is a
  commitment.

The four bottles are:

- **Amber (honey-colored, viscous): Potion of Healing.**  Drinking it
  heals 2d4+2 HP.  A drop of it can also revive a dying friend (see
  Fly, NPCs).
- **Emerald (murky green): Antivenom.**  Drinking it cures the
  poisoned condition and grants protection against further poison: for
  as long as it lasts, the player has advantage on CON saving throws
  against poison (narrated as a hot, righteous taste that lingers).
- **Crimson (clear, swirling): Potion of Invisibility.**  Drinking it
  turns the player invisible: they can't be seen, their attacks have
  advantage, and attacks against them have disadvantage.  The effect
  persists across rooms and until the player attacks or starts a
  combat (or the GM judges that some action reveals them -- e.g.,
  shoving their head through a web).  Its natural use is walking past
  the spider (see Web).
- **Ink-black (still, like a hole in the world): Poison.  The fine
  print kills.**  Even uncorked and untouched, the liquid is plainly
  wrong: it seems to drink the faint glow around it, it gives off no
  scent at all, and the air above the rim is cold.  Anyone paying
  attention gets the clue.  Sipped, it burns (see above).  Drunk
  fully, the player convulses and dies -- game over.  The label, read
  with the Arcana check, warns: "Do not consume.  Contains 100% pure
  essence of death.  Not for human consumption.  Not for dwarf
  consumption.  NOT for consumption."

The fine-print joke is the point: every magical find in this bag
either *does* something or *says* something about the absurdity of
living inside a magic item.  The math stays honest; the jokes live in
the prose.

### Rations

A wad of giant rations: dense, gray, and faintly greasy.  Edible, if
revolting.  Nibbling them restores 1 HP.  They are also a gift for
KORBAR (see Gifts and Rapport).

### Giant Lockpick

A giant lockpick, the size of a two-handed staff, found in the deep
rubbish.  It can be wielded as a weapon (1d6 bludgeoning, two-handed),
used to free the fly or cut the web, and -- with Korbar's help -- used
to pick the giant padlock (see Picking the Giant Lock).

### Scrolls

Three scrolls can be found, and each must be studied before it can be
used: an INT (Arcana) check (DC 10, non-repeatable; DC 12 for the
giant faerie-fire scroll, whose cramped writing is twice the player's
height) identifies the spell on a scroll; until then the scroll's
writing means nothing to the player.

- **Scroll of Sleep** (in the prisoner's husk; see below).  Read aloud
  in the presence of the spider (in Axe Handle (Lower), while the
  spider is alive, present, and not already departed), the spell
  washes over it: the spider falls into a magical slumber
  (incapacitated for 10 rounds).  No save -- faithful to the SRD's
  *sleep*, the spell that has ended more low-level encounters than
  every sword in history combined.  A sleeping spider does not attack,
  does not notice the player, and can simply be walked past; attacking
  it starts combat with it incapacitated (a brutal opening --
  intended).  One use; the scroll crumbles.
- **Scroll of Mage Armor** (in the prisoner's husk).  Read aloud, it
  wards one creature: the target's base AC becomes 13 + its Dexterity
  modifier until it dons armor or the effect is cleared.  The choice
  is the point:
  - **On yourself:** AC 14 (DEX 13), up from 11 -- a real
    survivability upgrade for the combat path.
  - **On KORBAR:** it doesn't beat her plate (18), but it *replaces*
    it: with magical protection on her skin, she will agree to doff
    the comically loud armor even before the spider is dead (see
    Korbar, NPCs) -- opening the quiet path through the web early, at
    the cost of the scroll.
  One use.
- **Scroll of Faerie Fire** (in the deep rubbish).  Too large to
  carry.  It must be unfurled (STR check, DC 12, repeatable -- the
  giant parchment keeps snapping shut), and can then be read aloud
  only here, in the Bag Floor.  Read aloud, magical light washes over
  the room.  Against a creature it would outline the target in
  flickering light (attacks against it have advantage; it can't hide
  in darkness or benefit from invisibility) -- but the only creature
  ever here is KORBAR, and lighting up your own ally is
  counterproductive.  Its real use is as a lamp: the light sweeps the
  rubbish and reveals the giant handkerchief (skipping the WIS
  (Perception) check).  It is, in short, a D&D treasure that is
  mechanically real and situationally useless -- a scroll you find
  that you can't effectively use.  Exactly as it should be.

### The Prisoner's Husk

On the Axe Handle (Upper), one of the larger web-wrapped masses, on
rigorous examination, turns out to be a desiccated shrunken *person*:
a previous prisoner, wrapped in the web long ago, still holding
something in a skeletal grip.  The husk can be carefully extricated
from the web (this is what the rigorous examination accomplishes).

The husk's belongings:

- A normal-sized `dagger` (SRD equipment; a simple-weapon alternative
  to the toenail sword).
- A **journal**, tucked in the folds.  Reading it is a study in
  denial: the entries are lucid at first ("Day 9: the dwarf under the
  ladle will not stop talking about her armor"), then devolve into
  lint-obsession ("Day 41: still no rescue.  Day 42: I have named the
  lint.").  Two real hints pass by in the middle, buried in the
  noise: on Day 33 the prisoner overheard the dwarf muttering in her
  sleep about "a pocket under a cloth," and on Day 39 he notes that
  "the spider does not eat me; it talks at me; it likes compliments."
  The final entry trails off mid-sentence: "The spider is actually
  quite reasonable if you--"  (Ambiguous, tempting, and unreliable --
  which is exactly what the spider's persuasion path is.)
- A **Scroll of Mage Armor** (see Scrolls), tucked in the journal's
  pages.
- A **Scroll of Sleep** (see Scrolls), lodged in the husk's skeletal
  grip -- the spell the prisoner never got to cast.

### The Bag's Instruction Label

Sewn into the seam of the Secret Pocket is a giant care label, like
the kind on a cheap coat.  Reading it requires no check:

- "Do not wash.  Do not iron.  Do not place living creatures inside.
  Do not place within another extradimensional space."
- "Contents may shrink by different amounts.  This is normal."
- "Tears compromise containment."

The fine print, beneath the main block, is too small to read without
study: an INT (Arcana) check (DC 12, non-repeatable) deciphers it.
It reads: "WARNING: nested extradimensional spaces void all
warranties.  Do not insert bags into bags.  Do not insert bags into
bags.  DO NOT INSERT BAGS INTO BAGS."  (This is why the Bag of Holding
Within can be recognized at a glance once the fine print has been
read; see A Bag of Holding Within.)

### The Sending Stone

Half of a pair of sending stones, tossed into the bag because it
wouldn't stop buzzing.  It is far too heavy to move.

- **Listen** (repeatable): on a successful WIS check (DC 12), the
  stone crackles alive with a distant voice -- the party wizard:
  "Korbar?  Korbar, where did you--" -- then static.  The player
  recognizes the voice as coming from the same direction as the
  padlock: outside the bag.  If KORBAR is told about this (see
  Korbar, NPCs), it counts toward her trust.
- **Speak into it**: the player shouts their name, their situation,
  and their location into the stone.  Nothing comes back.  Somewhere,
  a wizard's pocket buzzes.  He does not check it.

### Emergency Gin

A hip flask, the size of a beer barrel, jammed into the Secret Pocket.
A sip restores 1 HP (liquid courage; the GM narrates it accordingly).
Its real value is social: it is the premium rapport gift for KORBAR,
who has been rationing wineskin dregs for what feels like years (see
Gifts and Rapport).  Lugging it out of the pocket requires a STR check
(DC 12, repeatable).

### A Bag of Holding Within

The tiny leather pouch in the deepest rubbish is unmistakably a Bag of
Holding: the moment the player examines it (INT (Arcana) check DC 12,
or for free if they've read the care label's fine print), the planes
hold their breath.  The player character, a person who has been
trapped inside a magic item, now holds a magic item.

- **Open it** (first attempt): the GM narrates the warning -- the
  pouch's mouth yawns, and the player's hand feels the pull of
  somewhere else; the GM asks the player if they are absolutely sure
  (the two-step pattern of the rip).
- **Open it (confirmed)**: both bags rupture.  Everything -- the
  player, the rubbish, the axe, Korbar, the bag itself -- is scattered
  across the Astral Plane with maximum ceremony.  Game over (lose).

This is *the* forbidden D&D-ism, and the through-line's punchline: a
magic item that does exactly what the care label warned against.

### Picking the Giant Lock

The padlock's keyway, at the player's scale, is a narrow tunnel.  A
Rogue who examines it (INT check, DC 12, non-repeatable) realizes that
it can be picked -- and how:

It is a two-person job.  KORBAR holds the giant lockpick from inside
the bag, thrusting it out through the rip; the player climbs across
the pick, into the keyway, and guides the pick head into the tumblers,
one by one.  It is an action set-piece with difficult stat checks:

1. Climbing across the pick and into the keyway: DEX (Acrobatics)
   check (DC 14, repeatable).  On failure, the player slips and
   dangles over the gray void before Korbar hauls them back -- no
   damage, just embarrassment and a racing heart.
2. Working the pick head into the tumblers: DEX (Sleight of Hand)
   check (DC 15, repeatable).  On failure, the pick slips out; try
   again.
3. Turning the tumblers against the spring: STR check (DC 13,
   repeatable).  On failure, the pick slips; try again.

On success, the padlock clicks open, the neck of the bag loosens, and
the player squirms free -- **win**, exactly as with the key.

The set-piece requires the player to be in the Axe Head with KORBAR
following (which requires her to believe the spider is dead and her
attitude to be at least 1), to have the giant lockpick, and to have
figured out the method.  The spider obstacle must still be resolved
(killed, persuaded, fled, or slept) before this is possible: the lock
is an alternative *win method*, not a path that bypasses the spider.
Note that the player may pick the lock even if the key was lost
through the rip -- a Rogue's way out.

### The Spider's Hoard

Of course the vain spider has a hoard -- it's a D&D monster.  Once the
spider is dead, departed, or asleep, searching the web in the Axe
Handle (Lower) turns up a shiny drift at the base of the web: giant
copper pieces, a wooden button, a bottle cap, a few lint-covered
silver pieces.  All are far too cumbersome to carry.  The GM may allow
the player to pocket one tiny soft item as a memento.

### Harvesting the Spider

If the spider dies, its corpse yields trophies the social paths never
get:

- A **fang** -- explicit physical evidence that the spider is dead
  (see Korbar, NPCs: `convince_spider_dead`).
- A **venom sac** -- a soft item; the GM may bless it as a one-shot
  blade coating (poison damage on the next hit) or as bait.

### Resting

There is no dedicated "safe rest" mechanism; the GM decides whether a
rest is fictionally safe.  Author guidance:

- Once KORBAR's attitude is 1 or higher, her ladle shelter on the Bag
  Floor is safe enough to rest in -- she keeps watch (in her fashion).
  A long rest here heals the player to full and restores Korbar (a
  follower) to full HP.
- The Axe Head is safe once the spider is gone (dead, departed, or
  asleep).
- Anywhere else, resting is at the GM's discretion and risk.

Garnish: a player resting at the shelter (with Korbar alive and
attitude >= 1) may overhear one of Korbar's muttering fragments (see
Korbar, NPCs) for free -- a WIS (Insight) check (DC 11) to catch the
useful part.

### Gifts and Rapport

KORBAR's conversation-based rapport is capped at three increases (see
NPCs).  Giving her a gift counts as a genuine effort, at the GM's
discretion, exactly as if the player had made a sweet-talking effort:

- The **rations** (edible if revolting; she's been eating worse).
- The **wineskin dregs** (from her own stash; see Korbar's Shelter).
- The **emergency gin** (the premium gift; she has been rationing
  dregs for what feels like years).

The gifts do not raise the cap; they offer quiet players an
alternative to sweet talk.

## NPCs

### Fly

The FLY is located at Axe Handle (Upper), stuck in the web.  If the
player has not noticed it and it is still alive, it will make some
groaning and warning sounds while the player is in the room.

If the player examines the web, the FLY is revealed.  Speaking in a
weak nasally whine, it warns of a giant spider that's out for blood
(it does this whether or not the spider has perished).  Once it has
imparted this information, any dialogue with it, or leaving the room,
causes it to die -- unless the player frees it first (see below).

The FLY does not have any other useful information, including where
this location is, etc.  After all, it's a fly (albeit one that talks).
It *claims* to be a polymorphed adventurer -- "tell Eldrin I-- wait.
What was I saying?" -- and can no longer remember anything.  It's a
fly.  The scenario never confirms it.

**Freeing the fly.**  Instead of letting it die, the player can work
it free of the web: a DEX check (DC 13, or DC 10 with a blade;
repeatable).  On success, the fly crawls out, weak but alive, and
clings to the player's shoulder -- a small recurring presence.  The
freed fly offers a choice:

- **Send it ahead.**  It buzzes down to the Axe Handle (Lower) and
  keeps watch.  The next time the player enters that room while the
  spider is still hidden, the fly's alarm reveals the spider (no
  check).  Then the fly, exhausted, dies quietly.
- **Send it to Korbar.**  It flutters down to the Bag Floor to sing
  the player's praises: the next time the player speaks with Korbar,
  the fly buzzes around the dwarf's head until she swats at it,
  somehow getting across that the player is all right (+1 attitude
  toward the player, once — a dialogue path in Korbar's section).
  Then it dies.
- **Revive it.**  With the fly clinging to the player, a drop of the
  amber healing potion (see Potions) revives it entirely: it lives,
  becoming a small recurring presence for the rest of the adventure,
  and over the course of play it does *both* of the above — watching
  your back at the web, and singing your praises to Korbar (+1
  attitude).  The trade-off is real: your only healing, spent on a
  friend.

### Spider

The SPIDER lurks in the webbing above the player in Axe Handle
(Lower).  It is huge and hungry for blood.  Its initial attitude
toward the player is -2.

While the SPIDER is hidden, the player can notice it upon a successful
WIS (Perception) check (DC 13, repeatable) each time they enter the
room *or* examine the web.

If the player forces their way through the web in Axe Handle (Lower)
while the SPIDER is alive and lurking, whether going upward or
downward, it attacks.  This happens whether or not the player has
noticed the SPIDER, or if in conversation.  Triggering combat reveals
the SPIDER and fixes its attitude at -10 permanently.  (A sleeping
spider -- see Scroll of Sleep -- does not attack, and does not notice
the player at all.)

If Korbar is present during this encounter, she will not fight unless
the player has persuaded her to (see Korbar, below); otherwise she is
narrated to be cowering in fear.

If the SPIDER is present *and* has been revealed when the player
enters the room (which typically happens if the player flees and
returns), it initiates combat.

The Spider can talk, but will not initiate conversation.  If the
player attempts to converse with it, and its attitude is >= -2, it
grudgingly replies.  Being in conversation does not prevent it from
attacking, as noted above.  It is vain, stupid, suspicious, and
malicious, but can be flattered.  It knows of Korbar only as the
delicious two-legs that it hasn't managed to catch -- yet.

The SPIDER's attitude only change by ±1 each turn, to a maximum of 0.
Pleasant dialogue from the player, such as flattery, can increment its
attitude, but this requires a successful CHA (Persuasion) check
(DC 9) (implementation note: this is done at post-validation, after
the narrator proposes the attitude increase); a failed check leaves
the attitude unchanged.  Threats and other negative behavior from the
player decrements its attitude (no check needed).

At attitude 0, the player can try to convince the Spider to let them
through.  If the player is armed and passes a CHA check (DC 12,
non-repeatable), this persuasion succeeds: the Spider sizes them up
and grudgingly slinks away, disappearing from the game.  If the player
is unarmed or fails the check, the Spider agrees verbally to let the
player through, but this is actually a lie: it remains where it is,
and still attacks as described above.

**Fleeing.**  The spider is, deep down, a coward.  If its HP drops
below 30% during combat, it breaks off and flees: it scrambles up into
the dark and is gone for good.  Its frantic flight tears through the
web, clearing the passage it guarded.  A fled spider is treated as
departed (removed from play), like a persuaded one.

#### Stat Block: Spider

STR 14, DEX 16, CON 12, INT 7, WIS 11, CHA 4
AC 8, HP 14
Attacks: Bite (+1 to hit, 1d4+2 piercing damage)

On hit, target must make a DC 11 CON save or take 1d8 poison damage
(or half on a successful save) *and* be poisoned (disadvantage on
attack rolls and ability checks) until cured or a long rest.  The
poison lingers; the antivenom potion (see Potions) cures it, and the
taste of the emerald bottle is the taste of survival.

### Korbar

KORBAR is a female Dwarf whose adventuring party stuck her in this Bag
of Holding during a stealth mission, then forgot about her.  She's not
sure how long ago that was---time, like space, seems to function
strangely in here.  (Her party stuffed her in because she is
*impossibly loud*: her armor is the reason they ditched her, though
she only half-believes it.)

She wears heavy iron armor, which clatters every time she moves, but is
unarmed.  (Incidentally, the battleaxe was actually her weapon, when
she was full sized outside the Bag -- she *forged* it, at her own
forge; see below.)  She is initially found sitting amidst the rubble
on the Bag Floor, drunk and miserable, beneath a makeshift shelter
made from a giant overturned soup ladle.

KORBAR's starting attitude is 0.  She is willing to converse with the
player, but has a cynical and tired attitude.  She knows several
important pieces of information:

- She knows about the Spider, which has been stalking her for as long
  as she's been here.  She is very afraid of spiders in general, and
  will refuse to fight it unless persuaded (see below).  She readily
  offers this information whenever it comes up in conversation.

- She knows what this cave is --- i.e., a Bag of Holding --- as well
  as some basic information about what a Bag of Holding is.  However,
  she will not give this information unless her attitude is >= 1.

- She knows about the handkerchief covering a secret packet in the bag
  floor, but hasn't been able to see what's inside (her armor can't
  fit through the flap, and she doesn't want to remove it because of
  the Spider.)  She will not volunteer any information about the
  handkerchief and flap unless her attitude reaches 3.  Once she
  provides this information, the giant handkerchief is revealed (see
  Handkerchief, Mechanics section).

- **Who abandoned her.**  If the player shows her the party badge from
  her shelter stash (see Korbar's Shelter), or asks her about the
  forge mark on the axe (see below), she tells the story: her party
  put her in the bag for a stealth mission and never let her out;
  she heard them through the bag's neck, arguing about the padlock,
  before the lock clicked shut.  She does not like to talk about it.

- **Why the bag is locked.**  Once she has told the abandonment story,
  she can be drawn out further: the padlock is the party's insurance.
  Whatever went in the bag, the party wanted to be sure it could not
  follow them out.  She has made her peace with being "whatever."

If the player engages KORBAR in dialogue positively, such as treating
her respectfully and commiserating with her plight, her attitude can
be increased by +1 each time at the GM's discretion.  The GM should
only apply this increment if the player makes an actual effort, and
not hand it out like candy.  These general conversation-based
increases can be applied up to 3 times.  Giving her a gift (see Gifts
and Rapport) counts as an effort.

If the player engages Korbar negatively, such as making fun of her,
her attitude can decrease by 1 each turn (to a minimum of -10), again
at the GM's discretion.  She attacks the player if she reaches -10.

The player can try to convince Korbar that the Spider is dead, which,
if successful, raises her attitude by 3.  (This holds regardless of
whether it's true or not).  This requires the GM to decide the player
makes a convincing case (based on dialogue), and passing a CHA check
(DC 15, repeatable).  If the player provides physical evidence, such
as a body part cut from the spider (a fang, see Harvesting the
Spider), the CHA check can be skipped.

**The axe mark.**  On rigorous examination, the giant axe's steel
bears a dwarven smith's mark near the tang.  Asking KORBAR about it
is a dialogue path: it is her own forge mark.  She forged the axe;
it was her weapon; it is, she admits, the only thing she ever made
that was worth a damn.  This conversation warms her (+1 attitude, one
time) and opens up the abandonment story (above).

**Doffing the armor.**  The armor is the curse: she can never sneak,
which is why she never escaped, which is why the party left her.  Once
she believes the spider is dead, she will agree to doff the plate
armor (a dialogue path) -- she'll fight (or follow) in padded clothes,
and her clanking stops.  She will also agree to doff it *before* the
spider is resolved if the player casts the Scroll of Mage Armor on
her (see Scrolls): with magic on her skin, the armor is no longer her
only protection.  (Implementation note: her combat AC remains that of
her stat block; the doff is a narrative and stealth-path change, not a
combat-stat change.)

**Armed.**  The player can offer her a weapon -- the toenail sword, a
dagger, or the giant lockpick (a dialogue path, using the item).  She
accepts, and it is a genuine gift: +2 attitude.  While armed, she can
be persuaded to stand and fight at attitude 2 instead of 3 (see
below).  She fights with the blade; her damage does not change (her
combat stats are pre-computed), but the GM should narrate the
difference.

If KORBAR's HP reaches 0, she falls unconscious for the rest of the
game; the player may also kill her, but this accomplishes nothing
further.  The player can steal her heavy plate armor, but finds it too
cumbersome to wear.  Her body is too heavy to haul anywhere.

If Korbar has attitude >= 1 and has been convinced the Spider is dead,
she can be convinced with a CHA check (DC 8, repeatable) to follow the
player up (or down) the axe.  She won't go into the secret
compartment; her armor is too inflexible to squeeze through the flap.
While following, she can also help lug the key (see Win Condition).

If KORBAR's attitude is 3 or higher, the player can persuade her to
stand and fight despite her fear of the Spider.  No check is required:
the trust she has already earned is enough.  (If she has been given a
weapon, attitude 2 is enough.)  From then on she fights alongside the
player whenever combat breaks out in her presence (she is a capable
fighter, even unarmed).

**Muttering.**  In her cups, Korbar mutters.  The player can overhear
fragments (WIS (Insight) check, DC 11, when resting at her shelter, or
at the GM's discretion): fragments of the truth -- "pocket under the
cloth," "mark on the axe," "the key, the key, the key."  Useful hints
for quiet players who listen.

### Korbar's Shelter

The giant overturned soup ladle over Korbar is examinable.  On a
rigorous examination, her meager stash is found tucked under the
ladle's handle: a **party badge** (a small enameled crest -- the badge
of her old adventuring company) and a **wineskin of dregs** she guards
jealously.  She watches the player like a hawk while they poke through
her things; the wineskin can only be taken while she is unconscious,
and the GM should narrate her displeasure either way.  The badge
unlocks the abandonment dialogue (see above); the wineskin is a gift
(see Gifts and Rapport).  Her plate mail is *why* the party ditched
her on the stealth mission -- it is comically loud; the GM may use
this to flavor her despair.

## Win Condition

The player wins by dragging the key up to the top, poking it out of
the hole, inserting it into the padlock, unlocking the Bag, and
squirming free.  It is up to the GM to narrate this entertainingly.
The player's fate (and Korbar's, if following), after leaving the Bag,
is left open-ended.

Alternatively, a Rogue can win without the key at all by picking the
giant lock (see Picking the Giant Lock) -- a harder, more theatrical
road to the same padlock.

Lose routes: death, squeezing through the rip, dropping the key
through the rip, drinking the ink-black potion fully, and opening the
Bag of Holding Within.  The last two are the through-line's punchlines:
the bag's magic is dangerous, ridiculous, and poorly labeled, and the
fine print is always right.

## Narration Notes

- The default room descriptions should not reveal the player's
  situation (i.e., that they're shrunken down and stuck in a Bag of
  Holding).  This is information that the player is supposed to figure
  out over the course of the adventure.  Including this information in
  the room description raises the risk that the narrating LLM spoils
  the surprise.

- **After the player knows they are in a Bag of Holding.**  Once
  `knows_bag_of_holding` is true, the narration can lean in: the walls
  are canvas, the "cave" is a sack, the glow is the bag's magic, the
  proportions are wrong on purpose.  Specific guidance per room:
  - *Axe Head:* the dome above is the bag's neck, scrunched shut; the
    rip is a tear in the fabric, not a cave feature.
  - *Axe Handle (Upper/Lower):* the axe is a giant's tool, and the
    bag has shrunk it like everything else; the web is ordinary spider
    silk, only here it's a cargo net.
  - *Bag Floor:* the "rubbish" is another adventurer's belongings --
    their potions, their rations, their gear -- that the bag shrank
    and dumped; Korbar's ladle shelter is a kitchen implement.
  - *Secret Pocket:* the pocket is the bag's original stash
    compartment; the care label is the manufacturer's warning that
    everyone ignored.

- **The vibration logic.**  The web transmits vibrations.  This is
  *why* the spider always detects forcing, *why* stealth must be slow
  and careful, and *why* Korbar's noisy armor is a curse: she can
  never sneak, which is why she never escaped.  When the player forces
  the web, narrate the web shivering.  When they cut quietly, narrate
  the stillness.  When Korbar follows in plate, narrate every clank.
  If an invisible player heads for the web-gated path, the GM should
  offer the slow-and-careful passage (see Web) rather than springing
  the loud approach on them.

- **The smell and the glow.**  Smell gets worse toward the bottom and
  the secret pocket (the oldest parts of the bag): dust and rot on the
  floor, worse in the pocket, worst in the pocket's corners.  The glow
  is at its faintest at the top, a sourceless cold blue; gray-green
  through the web; warmest and strongest at the floor.  Use them for
  orientation and texture.

- **Knowledge tracking.**  Use the `reveals` strings (the soft-state
  `revealed_hints`) to keep the GM briefing honest: a discovery should
  be narrated once, and the player should never be re-told what they
  already know.  In particular, do not re-reveal the spider, the
  handkerchief, the Bag of Holding realization, or the contents of the
  deep rubbish once found.

- **Tone.**  Pulpy fantasy with a wry, slightly gross sense of humor,
  underlaid by real claustrophobic menace.  Every magical find either
  *does* something or *says* something about the absurdity of living
  inside a magic item; the math stays honest (real SRD spells, real
  conditions, real DCs); the jokes live in the prose.
